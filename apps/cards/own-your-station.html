A great way to cultivate this is by using the par sheet provided to check all of your ingredients at the start of your shift so that you can understand priorities in restocking and know what is low (and might run out in a rush). It is also important to ensure you stock items before shift change to set the next barista up for success.

Making sure utensils are rinsed immediately will prevent food from drying to it (making it take double the time to clean it). Wiping off the counter will prevent drinks from slipping (or sticking) and spilling. Keeping the sink clean will allow you to be able to rinse dishes faster and cut down on clutter. At the moment, each one of these things may seem insignificant, but together in a rush they make a massive difference.

If things are hard to find, then it will take more time to get drinks done. In addition to knowing where everything is, another factor is proactive thinking. You'll discover that every second counts when you are in a rush on the barista station. You'll also discover that every barista has a slightly different flow. As you learn, figure out what flow works (and doesn't work) for YOU. Ask other baristas how they developed their flow, and advice on how to improve quicker.
